# Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC) — The Conflict Monitor and Attention Regulator

## Overview
The **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)** is part of the limbic system located in the medial frontal cortex. It plays a pivotal role in **attention**, **emotional regulation**, and **monitoring conflicts** during cognitive processes.

## Key Functions
- Detects **conflicts** between competing responses or thoughts.
- Monitors errors and signals need for increased cognitive control.
- Regulates **attention** and helps shift focus when needed.
- Involved in processing emotional and pain-related information.

## Role in Memory and Habit Formation
- Helps adjust behavior when habits conflict with goals or when errors occur.
- Supports **executive control** to override automatic habits when necessary.
- Facilitates switching between habitual and goal-directed behavior.
- Plays a role in **motivation and emotional processing**, influencing habit strength.

## Related Brain Areas
- **[Prefrontal Cortex](prefrontal_cortex.md)**: Works with ACC for executive functions and decision-making.
- **[Basal Ganglia](basal_ganglia.md)**: Habit formation and procedural memory.
- **[Amygdala](amygdala.md)**: Emotional processing and fear conditioning.

> ⚠️ Dysfunction in the ACC can lead to difficulties in controlling impulses, adapting habits, or managing conflicting goals.
