# Habit Loop — The Cycle of Automatic Behavior

## Overview
The **habit loop** is the core framework describing how habits form and persist. It consists of three components: **Cue**, **Routine**, and **Reward**.

## Key Points
- **Cue**: The trigger that starts the habit (e.g., time, location, emotional state).
- **Routine**: The behavior performed, which can be physical, mental, or emotional.
- **Reward**: The benefit or positive feedback that reinforces the behavior.

## Role in Memory Formation
- Habit loops shift behaviors from **conscious, effortful control** to **automatic procedural memory**.
- The **basal ganglia** plays a central role in storing and executing these habitual routines.
- Repetition strengthens neural pathways, embedding the loop in long-term memory.
- The **prefrontal cortex** is active during habit learning but less so once habits are automatic.

## Related Brain Areas
- **[Basal Ganglia](./basal_ganglia)**: Stores and executes habitual routines.
- **[Prefrontal Cortex](./prefrontal_cortex)**: Guides initial learning and planning.
- **[Hippocampus](./hippocampus)**: Stores context (time, place) related to habits.
